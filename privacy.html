Golf Handicap — Privacy Policy

Effective August 5, 2026

Golf Handicap runs entirely in your browser. This page sets out exactly what data exists, where it goes, and who can see it.

What the app stores

Only what you type in: golfer names, course names with their tee ratings, and for each round its date, score, course, tees, conditions adjustment and note — plus your settings (your handicap method, which figure is shown first, and the theme). There is no account, no profile, and no usage tracking of any kind. Nothing is required to use the app.

Where that data lives

In your browser (always). Everything is stored in your browser's local storage on your own device. If you never sign in, this is the only copy that exists — none of your data is ever transmitted anywhere. Clearing the site's browser data deletes it, which is why the app offers a JSON backup you can keep yourself.

Google sign-in / Firestore (optional, off until you choose it). If you tap “Sign in to sync”, your rounds are stored in a Firebase/Firestore database operated by the developer so the same data appears on your other devices, and Firebase Authentication records your Google account's email address and display name. Security rules confine every account to its own document — no other signed-in user can read yours. This only ever happens if you sign in; the app never does it on its own.

Backups you download. “Download backup” writes a JSON file straight to your device. It is never uploaded, and where it goes afterwards is entirely up to you. Note that it contains everything the app holds, in plain readable text.

What is never collected

  • No analytics, no advertising, no third-party trackers, no cookies for tracking.
  • No location data — a course is just a name and some numbers you typed.
  • No contacts, no photos, no device identifiers.
  • Nothing is sold or shared with anyone, in any circumstances.

Third parties

If, and only if, you sign in, the app talks to Google Firebase (Authentication and Firestore) on your behalf. Their handling of that data is covered by Firebase's privacy documentation. The page is served by GitHub Pages, which — like any web host — sees the ordinary request information involved in serving a page. Expanding the “Recent changes” list at the foot of the app fetches the list of updates from the GitHub API — an ordinary web request that carries none of your data, though like any request it shows GitHub your IP address. It only happens when you open that section.

Deleting your data

Signed out: clear this site's data in your browser, and it is gone. Signed in: sign in, remove the golfers you want removed, and the change is pushed to the stored copy. To have the whole stored document deleted, email eagleadams86@gmail.com from the Google account you signed in with, and it will be removed.

Children

The app is not directed at children under 13 and collects nothing that would identify one. Google sign-in is subject to Google's own age requirements.

Changes

If what the app stores or where it sends it ever changes, this page and its effective date change in the same release.
